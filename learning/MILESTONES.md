# Thirty milestones: collective spin repair

This map follows one scientific question: how much microscopic repair can
replenish a collective spin probe while independent local noise continues to
act? The selected educational anchor is **H.-P. Breuer and F. Petruccione,
*The Theory of Open Quantum Systems* (2002)**. Its Chapter 2 and Chapter 3,
Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3, supply the state, operation and Markovian-generator
foundations. The [source bridge](BRIDGE.md) translates those foundations into
the notation used here. Collective-spin representation theory, phase QFI and
the repair frontier are developed in the project's own scientific pages;
they are not attributed to the anchor's cited sections.

The allocation is **12 background/preliminaries milestones and 18
narrative/innovation milestones**. The first phase joins a single spin,
open-system evolution and a collective observable into the complete operational
model. The second begins with the local noise and repair calculations, then
assembles the universal converse, its matching preparation, and their physical
meaning. The larger second phase reflects the need to understand both sides
of a resource frontier and the assumptions that make them comparable.

Dependencies refer to earlier milestones. They describe the logical structure
of the science. Specialist details remain in the linked proofs; the map
does not attempt to enumerate every calculation.

## Phase I — Background/preliminaries

### M01 — One noisy spin and its polarization

- **Central question:** What information does a spin-half density operator retain about a physical sensor?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret $`\rho=(I+\mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2`$ as a state with a Bloch polarization, and relate Pauli expectation values to components of $`\mathbf r`$. Distinguish a mixed state from a smaller spin Hilbert space. The unpolarized spin remains a physical sensor even when its individual mean spin vanishes.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Entry point. Establish the one-sensor object before introducing a many-spin resource.
- **Conceptual connection:** Local polarization will be generated by a counted pump; the eventual probe can also use spins that remain individually unpolarized.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2; [Model](../science/model.md), Section 1; [Construction](../science/construction.md), Section 1.

### M02 — A sensor inside a larger quantum system

- **Central question:** How can a protected register assist without becoming an extra sensing carrier?
- **Understanding target:** Distinguish a joint sensor–auxiliary state from the physical sensor's reduced state, including entangled cases. Identify which system receives noise and which system later receives the phase. A protected auxiliary may retain correlations or support control, while the signal generator still acts only on the $`N`$ physical spins.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M01. Extend the one-spin description to the composite setting needed for a converse that permits ancillary assistance.
- **Conceptual connection:** Operator identities on the sensors remain identities after tensoring with an auxiliary identity; dimension alone does not change the relevant commutators.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2; [Model](../science/model.md), Sections 1–3; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Sections 2 and 5.

### M03 — Rates in a local Lindblad generator

- **Central question:** What does the preparation noise rate $`\kappa`$ mean operationally?
- **Understanding target:** Read the Hamiltonian and dissipative parts of a finite-dimensional master equation, including the convention $`\mathcal D[L](\rho)=L\rho L^\dagger-\lbrace L^\dagger L,\rho\rbrace /2`$. Explain why jump amplitudes contain square roots of rates. Under the project's independent isotropic depolarization, each single-spin Bloch component decays at rate $`\kappa`$.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M01–M02. Give the local noise a calibrated meaning before comparing it with repair strength.
- **Conceptual connection:** A factor in the generator changes both the decay time and the dimensionless repair ratio $`B/\kappa`$.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Model](../science/model.md), Section 1; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 1.

### M04 — Following an observable instead of every state component

- **Central question:** Why does the adjoint generator help establish a bound for every allowed state?
- **Understanding target:** Relate the Schrödinger-picture state equation to the Heisenberg-picture identity $`d\langle O\rangle/dt=\langle\mathcal L^\dagger(O)\rangle`$. Distinguish an operator identity from a relation checked for one initial state. Understand how a closed evolution law for one observable can survive an otherwise complicated controlled many-body state.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M03. Introduce the method that reduces the eventual all-control argument to one sensor moment.
- **Conceptual connection:** The proof will follow total spin length rather than solve the complete density matrix or infer a relaxation gap.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Sections 1–4.

### M05 — Collective spin and the full sector structure

- **Central question:** What is retained by $`C=S_x^2+S_y^2+S_z^2`$ beyond the mean spin?
- **Understanding target:** Form $`S_a=\frac12\sum_i\sigma_i^a`$ from physical spins and interpret the Casimir eigenvalues $`j(j+1)`$. Distinguish the complete $`2^N`$-dimensional spin space from the maximal-spin sector. A permutation-invariant state need not be confined to that sector, and zero mean spin does not imply zero second moment.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M01–M04. Move from local sensor variables to the collective resource that the proof tracks.
- **Conceptual connection:** Independent local noise can change sector populations even when its complete generator treats all spins identically.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for composite states; [Model](../science/model.md), Sections 1–2; [Interpretation](../science/interpretation.md), “Spin length and useful orientation are separate resources.”

### M06 — The individual commutator condition for supplied controls

- **Central question:** Which control operations preserve the physical spin-sector resource?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret $`[H_0,C]=[J_\nu,C]=0`$ as conditions on every supplied Hamiltonian and jump. Explain why these are stronger and more specific than assigning a symmetry to the complete channel. The allowed class includes powerful nonlinear and sensor–auxiliary controls, provided they preserve the sensor Casimir itself.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M02, M04 and M05. Identify the free-control boundary before any repair cost is defined.
- **Conceptual connection:** Control can change orientation and entanglement inside a sector without replenishing the sector moment; this separates two useful resources.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Model](../science/model.md), Section 2; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 1.

### M07 — Why dissipative second moments contain an extra term

- **Central question:** How does a Lindblad jump act differently on $`O^2`$ and on $`O`$?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret the dissipative product identity as an anticommutator contribution plus a positive commutator-square remainder. A dissipator is not a Hamiltonian derivation. Understand the roles of both terms before they are bounded, and why omitting the remainder would change a resource estimate for a second moment.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M03–M04. Establish one generic operation-level ingredient needed for the later local-repair calculation.
- **Conceptual connection:** The anticommutator produces a moment-dependent repair term; the positive remainder produces the bounded-locality correction.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3 for the generator; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 2 for the project-supplied product identity.

### M08 — Support, norm and an instantaneous repair budget

- **Central question:** What exactly is counted when a repair term touches several sensors and an auxiliary?
- **Understanding target:** Read $`|X|\|h_X\|`$ and $`|X|\|L_{X\mu}\|^2`$ as support-weighted contributions with units of inverse time. The support counts physical sensors, including nonadjacent ones. Ancillary coupling is allowed under the same norm rule. The budget belongs to the supplied decomposition and obeys a hard cap at each time.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M02–M03 and M06–M07. Turn the control distinction into the resource contract used by both bounds.
- **Conceptual connection:** A small long-time average cannot rule out an intense terminal repair burst; the pointwise cap will do essential work.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Model](../science/model.md), Section 2; [Resources](../science/resources.md), “Charged repair.”

### M09 — A prepared probe's local phase sensitivity

- **Central question:** Which aspect of a sensing experiment does phase QFI quantify here?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret the family $`e^{-i\theta G}\rho e^{i\theta G}`$ for a fixed unit homogeneous spin axis and its symmetric-logarithmic-derivative QFI. Distinguish that local phase metric from a global estimation risk, repetition count or noisy frequency-sensing protocol. Identify the variance and generator spectral range as useful upper-bound ingredients.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M02 and M05. Connect the spin resource to the declared output while keeping the sensing task precise.
- **Conceptual connection:** The signal acts on the physical spins even when an auxiliary is retained; a useful collective moment constrains its assisted QFI.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for states and measurement probabilities; project-supplied QFI in [Model](../science/model.md), Section 3, and [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 5.

### M10 — Preparation records and phase ordering

- **Central question:** What changes when a preparation outcome is kept for later readout?
- **Understanding target:** Describe a classical–quantum output as outcome probabilities with conditional quantum states. Explain why records generated before the phase have parameter-independent probabilities, so their phase QFI is the probability-weighted conditional QFI. Distinguish keeping this record from extracting information throughout a parameter-dependent sensing history.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M02 and M09. Add the output information needed to interpret feedback and heralding fairly.
- **Conceptual connection:** A rare branch can have a sensitive probe without supplying that sensitivity at unit yield.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for operations and outcomes; [Model](../science/model.md), Section 6; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 6.

### M11 — Initial resources and a prescribed output time

- **Central question:** Why must the readout deadline accompany a claim about long preparation?
- **Understanding target:** Recognize $`x_0=\operatorname{Tr}(C\rho(0))`$ as an initially supplied physical resource, potentially of order $`N^2`$. Distinguish an arbitrary finite-time bound, a stationary consequence and a bound uniform after initial resources have decayed. The output time is prescribed; it is not chosen by waiting for a favorable outcome.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M04–M05 and M08–M10. Add the temporal contract that makes different preparation protocols comparable.
- **Conceptual connection:** The logarithmic-in-$`N`$ horizon will remove an initially quadratic term without assuming a unique steady state.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Model](../science/model.md), Sections 3–6; [Resources](../science/resources.md), “Why the time and output conventions matter.”

### M12 — The complete preparation problem

- **Central question:** Which resources and output define the frontier to be established?
- **Understanding target:** Assemble the $`N`$ noisy sensors, protected register, arbitrary supplied Casimir-preserving controls, bounded-support repair decomposition and instantaneous cap into one protocol. State the deterministic preparation interval and subsequent unit homogeneous phase imprint together. Identify which resources are constrained and which powerful collective operations are explicitly supplied.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Integrates M01–M11. Complete the background phase with an operational model rather than another isolated prerequisite.
- **Conceptual connection:** The universal upper bound and the attaining construction must use this same noise, budget, time and output contract.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapters 2–3 through the [source bridge](BRIDGE.md); [Model](../science/model.md), Sections 1–6; [Resources](../science/resources.md).

## Phase II — Narrative/innovation

### M13 — One-site and two-site Pauli decay

- **Central question:** Which part of a collective second moment is exposed to independent noise?
- **Understanding target:** Follow a single Pauli expectation decaying at rate $`\kappa`$ and a Pauli product on distinct sensors decaying at rate $`2\kappa`$. Separate these correlations from identity contributions. Explain why independent local depolarization has this support-dependent action even when the initial state is correlated or nonsymmetric.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M03–M05 and M12. Begin the research argument with its smallest noise calculation.
- **Conceptual connection:** The Casimir contains only an identity term and these two-site products, allowing an exact closed balance without solving the full state.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 1, equations (1)–(3).

### M14 — A Casimir identity immune to the supplied processing

- **Central question:** How can a noise identity survive arbitrary powerful free control?
- **Understanding target:** Combine the Pauli expansion of $`C`$ with $`\mathcal N_\kappa^\dagger(C)=-2\kappa(C-3NI/4)`$. Account separately for Hamiltonians, individual jumps and inserted free channels commuting with the sensor Casimir. The identity remains valid with protected auxiliaries and does not require the state to remain in a symmetric subspace.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M06 and M13. Elevate a local decay calculation into a control-independent observable statement.
- **Conceptual connection:** Free processing can regenerate useful orientation within current sectors while contributing exactly zero to this particular resource balance.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 1; [Interpretation](../science/interpretation.md), first section.

### M15 — Exact moment depletion without repair

- **Central question:** What survives indefinitely when no charged control changes the sectors?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret the exact solution $`x(t)=3N/4+(x_0-3N/4)e^{-2\kappa t}`$. Separate the decaying initial resource from the linear background. A stationary state must have moment $`3N/4`$, but the argument does not establish a full stationary density matrix, uniqueness or a relaxation time for every observable.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M04, M11 and M14. Extract the first complete dynamical consequence while retaining its exact scope.
- **Conceptual connection:** A bound on the spin moment can obstruct quadratic homogeneous sensitivity even when the controller remains powerful inside each sector.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Sections 1 and 4; [Model](../science/model.md), equations (10)–(11).

### M16 — What one microscopic repair term can change

- **Central question:** How does physical support limit a local repair's action on collective spin?
- **Understanding target:** Split $`S_a`$ into its supported and complementary parts and use $`\|S_{X,a}\|=|X|/2`$ to bound their commutator. Apply this to a Hamiltonian term and to a jump's adjoint action. An arbitrarily large protected auxiliary does not enlarge the supported physical spin operator's norm.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M07–M08 and M14. Introduce the elementary repair estimate before summing over a complete controller.
- **Conceptual connection:** The support-weighted budget has been chosen to control these commutators, including coherent repair and ancillary purity transfer.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3 for adjoint dynamics; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 2.

### M17 — The square-root repair drift

- **Central question:** Why does the universal repair contribution scale as $`B\sqrt{x}`$ rather than $`BN`$?
- **Understanding target:** Collect the first-moment repair operators into $`A_a=\mathcal R^\dagger(S_a)`$, with $`\|A_a\|\le B`$. Connect the anticommutator expectation to weighted operator Cauchy–Schwarz, then combine the three spin components. Track the positive jump remainder separately as the $`3kB`$ correction.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M07, M08 and M16. Assemble the local estimates into the load-bearing inequality for an arbitrary many-body state.
- **Conceptual connection:** Keeping the actual moment in the bound is what produces the intermediate quadratic repair scale; a rough maximum-norm bound would lose it.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3 for the generator; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 3.

### M18 — An all-time bound without a stationary assumption

- **Central question:** How does a differential repair inequality constrain an arbitrary prescribed endpoint?
- **Understanding target:** Combine the exact noise loss and repair drift, use Young's inequality, and follow the integrating-factor comparison. Interpret the surviving $`e^{-\kappa T}x_0`$ term and the bracket involving $`N`$, $`(B/\kappa)^2`$ and $`kB/\kappa`$. Explain why inserted free operations do not interrupt the moment comparison.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M11, M15 and M17. Convert a local-in-time estimate into the universal finite-time resource statement.
- **Conceptual connection:** A stationary upper bound follows as one consequence; it is not a premise needed to restrict fast or time-dependent controllers.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 4; [Model](../science/model.md), Theorem 1.

### M19 — From spin moment to the homogeneous phase bound

- **Central question:** How does the moment inequality become a sensitivity frontier?
- **Understanding target:** Join $`F_Q\le4\operatorname{Var}(G)\le4\langle C\rangle`$ with the independent spectral cap $`F_Q\le N^2`$. At $`T\ge\log(N+2)/\kappa`$, explain why the arbitrary initial contribution is at most linear. Keep fixed locality $`k`$, the physical sensor Casimir and the subsequent homogeneous phase in the statement.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M09, M12 and M18. Complete the converse for the actual output quantity.
- **Conceptual connection:** Protected ancillary assistance changes the state and readout possibilities, while the generator range and moment bound still constrain its QFI.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for composite states; project-supplied argument in [Converse](../science/converse.md), Section 5, and [Model](../science/model.md), Sections 4–5.

### M20 — Records, yield and success-selected waiting

- **Central question:** Which preparation advantages survive averaging at one prescribed time?
- **Understanding target:** Extend the drift through finitely many prescribed feedback interventions with finite outcomes and the cap enforced on every branch. Interpret the recorded output QFI and the accepted yield $`p_{\rm acc}F_Q`$. Contrast this fixed-time quantity with waiting until a maximal-spin outcome eventually occurs, whose random completion time has its own cost.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M10–M11 and M18–M19. Complete the universal upper statement's outcome and timing conventions before constructing a matching endpoint.
- **Conceptual connection:** Concavity of the square root bounds average repair drift; retaining a rare outcome does not remove its probability from the resource account.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for operations and outcomes; [Converse](../science/converse.md), Sections 6–7; [Model](../science/model.md), Section 6.

### M21 — A local pump under persistent depolarization

- **Central question:** What finite polarization can a counted repair budget actually maintain?
- **Understanding target:** For each selected sensor, combine the pump $`\sqrt\kappa|0\rangle\langle1|`$ with depolarization to obtain $`\dot p=\kappa-2\kappa p`$. Starting from maximally mixed sensors, identify the exact finite-time polarization and the cost $`m\kappa`$ for $`m`$ pumped spins. The limiting value $`1/2`$ is not reached at a finite preparation time.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M03, M08 and M12. Begin the lower construction with an operation counted under the same instantaneous budget as the converse.
- **Conceptual connection:** Repair supplies a number of partly polarized sensors proportional to $`B/\kappa`$, rather than an uncharged pure ensemble.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Construction](../science/construction.md), Section 1; [Resources](../science/resources.md), construction ledger.

### M22 — A sector-preserving cat operation

- **Central question:** How can supplied collective processing amplify the polarization without changing total spin?
- **Understanding target:** Identify $`P=X^{\otimes N}`$ as a global spin rotation up to phase, so $`[P,C]=0`$ and $`e^{-i\pi P/4}`$ is allowed. Follow how conjugating $`S_z`$ couples complementary computational-basis strings. Being admitted to the free class does not make the direct many-body Hamiltonian inexpensive to realize.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M05–M06, M09 and M21. Add the collective operation to the locally prepared mixed input.
- **Conceptual connection:** The same controller that cannot replenish the depleted moment can reshape an existing moment into a useful homogeneous-phase probe.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for unitary operations; [Construction](../science/construction.md), Section 2; [Resources](../science/resources.md), “Free controls are supplied resources.”

### M23 — Complementary strings and mixed-state QFI

- **Central question:** How do the mixed input eigenvalues determine the cat probe's sensitivity?
- **Understanding target:** Relate a string's eigenvalue ratio to $`\tanh(\operatorname{atanh}(p)K)`$, where $`K`$ sums the pumped signs. Combine complementary-string matrix elements with the unpumped spins' conditional variance to obtain the exact QFI expectation. Explain why mixed spins still contribute and why both parities of $`N`$ are covered.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M09 and M21–M22. Establish the quantitative lower-construction quantity before using its scaling.
- **Conceptual connection:** This calculation uses an inherited mixed-state amplification mechanism; its role here is to match a specifically priced preparation task.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for spectral state descriptions; project derivation in [Construction](../science/construction.md), Section 2; [Related work](../references/RELATED_WORK.md), “Mixed-state amplification.”

### M24 — A uniform lower bound from a finite polarization supply

- **Central question:** Why does pumping $`m`$ spins give the order $`N+m^2`$?
- **Understanding target:** Use the integer-valued $`K`$, the event $`K\ne0`$ and its second moment to interpret the bound $`F_{p,m}\ge p^4(N+m^2)`$ for $`0\lt p\le1/2`$. Connect $`m=\lfloor B/\kappa\rfloor`$ to a uniform lower order when $`B\ge\kappa`$. Keep the finite-time positive polarization lower bound explicit.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M17–M19 and M21–M23. Reveal how the local pump and collective amplification reach the same intermediate scale as the converse.
- **Conceptual connection:** The quadratic term counts the maintained polarization supply, while the linear term can use the rest of the mixed ensemble.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for the probabilistic state description; [Construction](../science/construction.md), Section 3; [Interpretation](../science/interpretation.md), mixed-probe example.

### M25 — A finite pulse while noise remains active

- **Central question:** Can the construction fit the deterministic deadline without switching off depolarization?
- **Understanding target:** Interpret $`H_{\rm pulse}=\Omega X^{\otimes N}`$, its duration and the trace-distance error bound $`\delta\le\kappa N\tau`$. Allocate the preparation interval between finite pumping and the pulse. The sufficient choice leaves a pumping duration at least $`1/\kappa`$ at every advertised output time, while the pulse strength may grow with $`N`$.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M03, M11 and M21–M24. Replace an instantaneous-operation shorthand with a finite controlled process under continuing noise.
- **Conceptual connection:** The error estimate prices time and noise exposure; it does not supply a cheap implementation of the many-body Hamiltonian.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Construction](../science/construction.md), Section 4; [Resources](../science/resources.md), pulse ledger.

### M26 — A fixed-measurement robustness witness

- **Central question:** Why does a small pulse error preserve a useful fraction of phase sensitivity?
- **Understanding target:** Use the ideal probe's SLD, its norm bound and its phase-dependent mean to compare the actual derivative and variance with the ideal values. Interpret the resulting classical FI lower bound and its constant-fraction consequence. This argument does not assume a uniform QFI continuity law near small state eigenvalues.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M09, M23 and M25. Complete the finite-pulse lower bound with an explicit measurement-level reason.
- **Conceptual connection:** A known measurement furnishes a lower witness, while QFI optimizes over measurements. Its mathematical availability does not establish an efficient physical readout.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 2 for measurement probabilities; [Construction](../science/construction.md), Section 4, equations (5)–(7); [Resources](../science/resources.md).

### M27 — The linear floor with no charged repair

- **Central question:** What can the full supplied free class do when $`B`$ is arbitrarily small?
- **Understanding target:** Describe the idempotent channel resetting each irreducible spin register to an extremal cat while retaining multiplicity. Its Kraus operators preserve $`C`$. Combine the exact linear moment with $`j^2\ge j(j+1)/3`$ and a finite-rate robustness estimate to understand the order-$`N`$ floor, including $`B=0`$.
- **Dependencies and story role:** Uses M05–M07, M15, M19 and M26. Close the lower construction's small-budget range without inventing an uncharged local pump.
- **Conceptual connection:** These global, highly structured jumps and their growing supplied rate are allowed resources, not a local-reservoir implementation or a stationary quadratic-QFI construction.
- **Source pointers:** Anchor Chapter 3, Sections 3.2.1–3.2.3; [Construction](../science/construction.md), Section 5; [Resources](../science/resources.md), low-budget baseline.

### M28 — The complete repair frontier and two architectural scales
